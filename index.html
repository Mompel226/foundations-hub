About this picture

Real

  • The body and her organs: the Visible Human Female, a real woman's body that was cut into thin slices and photographed for a medical atlas (US National Library of Medicine). The organs were traced for the HuBMAP Human Reference Atlas.
  • The tissue: two photographs of the lining of a cervix, taken through a light microscope.
  • The cell and its organelles: measured by an electron microscope. The shape, size and place of the cell, its nucleus, mitochondria, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, lysosomes, endosomes and lipid droplets; the place of every ribosome, vesicle and pore in the nucleus; the path of every microtubule; where the two centrioles are and which way they point.

Drawn from what is known

  • The shape of a ribosome, a pore in the nucleus and a centriole: the microscope shows where each one is; the shape is drawn at its real size from other studies. A vesicle is a ball of the volume that was measured; a microtubule is a tube 25 nm wide along the path that was measured.
  • The molecules are a model. Measured, and used: the shape and size of each kind of protein (Protein Data Bank), how many of each kind a HeLa cell holds (Mueller et al. 2020), about 3 million protein molecules in every cubic micrometre (Milo 2013), kinesin's 8 nm steps. Made up: where each molecule sits, the kinesin's stalk, and the speed. Kinesin takes about 100 steps a second; here it takes one. At that speed the other molecules would be a blur, so here they move only when the vesicle reaches them.

Not the same person

  • The body, the tissue and the cell come from three different people. The cell is a HeLa cell grown in a laboratory, not a cell from this body.

Not shown

  • Water and ions, and at the organelle level almost all the proteins: too small to see at that zoom, and far too many to draw.
  • Movement inside the cell. It was frozen, so the cell is one moment. In a living cell all of it moves, all the time.

Chosen

  • The colours of the 3D parts: most of the parts of a cell are smaller than a wave of light, so they have no colour of their own. The haze shows how far away something is.

The computer that found each part of the cell makes some mistakes, so a few small pieces are missing or wrongly named.
